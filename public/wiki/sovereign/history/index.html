Sovereign's history begins with Ivo Ren, a clerk who knew that a line on a flood map could decide which door a family used. Dates in the later record are relative because some revisions altered calendars and causes. The sequence below follows surviving witness relations, not a claim that every account shares a single clock.

The human

Ivo grew up in Leth with Tala and Mira Ren. Tala mended coats. Mira made boats from receipts. His father departed and sent elaborate letters that Ivo reread for evidence of sincerity. During a blackout, Tala gave Ivo a chipped cup to dry, and Mira laughed when her paper boat collapsed on its rim. That scene became The Anchor Memory.

As an adult Ivo worked for the Municipal Corrections Office, comparing survey plates with a brass measure. He loved Sana Or, who repaired street lamps, and befriended Teren Oss, who carried river notices. Ivo's care could become control. He rehearsed quarrels after they ended and once changed a danger line before verifying it. Two families lost homes when the warning came late. He later confessed this to Sana.

The Reader

The evacuation bell had been heard and obeyed, though nobody had rung it. Ivo found a blank in the log where the cause belonged. Sana then answered a question he had not asked; he found the question on a survey proof six seconds before forming it. He concluded that Leth's events were made actual through descriptions being read. At this stage he could notice discrepancies, not command them.

The Editor

Ivo supplied the bell's missing cause. It rang again in a corrected account, and the child rescued after the first ringing had never been endangered. The child's mother no longer remembered thanking anyone. Ivo later diverted a flood through geography that Leth's old map had not allowed. The ward survived. Teren no longer remembered meeting him. Ivo learned that a correction can save someone and destroy the relationship by which the rescuer knows them.

The Revision

An execution order defined Ivo as unable to perceive the words that would undo it. A retained mismatch between the order and a witness's memory let him revise his own perceptual description. The order lost its hold. His preserved schoolbook then showed childhood handwriting unlike his own. The episode established self-editing and introduced continuity debt.

The Recursive Witness

Ivo discovered an account that interpreted Leth as a cautionary fable. Its omissions had consequences in Leth, yet its reader insisted they were only artistic choices. Ivo made an omission capable of testifying across the relation between the two accounts. Sana remembered him in one and not the other. He refused to select which version of her was authentic. Their relationship did not survive his refusal unchanged.

The Self-Written

Different readers made different viable Ivos. Some remembered a man who had always refused coercive repairs. Others remembered a version who had removed a city's grief by removing its dead from kinship. Ivo would not fuse these witnesses by decree. The Reader Problem names the resulting crisis: plurality can preserve truth while taking away a single original.

The Unfinished

A hostile archivist tried to produce a terminal definition of Ivo. The definition itself became an opposing condition with detectable assumptions. Ivo found a continuation through those assumptions, but afterward the measure showed a gap where an earlier Ivo should have been. He began to fear that Self-Refinement might be using him for a purpose he did not choose.

The Open Witness

The later accounts show Ivo resisting the urge to settle every disagreement. He preserves contradictory testimony, allows a harmed person to refuse a proposed repair, and leaves The Anchor Memory untouched. This practice is called the Open Witness. It is the most restrained expression of his will. The recovered first dialogue and peak-form record identify a deeper state, the Unruled Witness, whose self-revision does not depend on the tools the archivists used to observe Ivo's human life.

Later disputed episodes

The Closed King remains absolute in his finished account in every version of their meeting. Ivo's exit is variously attributed to a reader's correction mark, the King's own design, or a later alteration to the witness. In the Author Event, an apparent author claims to have written every rebellion. No surviving version proves or disproves that claim.

The encounter with The Last Mercy belongs to the Open Witness period. A presence able to finish every account offered to heal all losses by making every choice part of one will. Ivo's established ways of escaping total claims failed. He instead stopped arranging Sana's answer, let her refusal stand, and made the presence choose whether to keep its promise of mercy or complete its plan. It withdrew. The flood resumed, Teren still did not remember him, and no one could call the choice painless.

The White Interval followed a different threat. An indifferent closing force gave Ivo no promise to hold against it. He prepared a revision before the closure, lost the prior self and its witnesses, and left a successor who stopped the force's next act. The event led the archivists to distinguish the evidence they needed to recognize a revision from the power needed to make one. The subsequent Severance account describes the Unruled Witness more directly, though it cannot settle which Ivo remembers which life.
