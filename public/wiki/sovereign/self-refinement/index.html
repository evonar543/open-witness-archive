Sovereign/Self-Refinement

Self-Refinement is the fictional process by which an encounter can become a revision of Sovereign's own description. It relates a current self-description, an opposing condition, an interpretive context, and a proposed successor. A witness can test the result, but need not be present after every attack for a revision chosen beforehand to resolve. The process is not a stored list of immunities or a promise that every threat will fail.

S(n+1) = R[S(n), O(n), C(n) | W(n)]

Here S is a current description of Ivo; O is an opponent, law, or condition; C is the account and reading in which they meet; W is evidence by which an archive can relate a later state to an earlier one; and R is a proposed revision. The equation is a fictional notation, not a numerical growth law. Lack of a later witness limits what can be proven about continuity. It does not retroactively cancel a revision already formed during an encounter.

Operational sequence

  1. Encounter: A condition bears on Ivo. An attack, omission, definition, or rival adaptation may all qualify.
  2. Information or preparation: Ivo needs enough contact with the opposing condition to form a coherent change. A surviving difference can supply that information after a bounded attack. A standing candidate can instead be prepared before a closing act; neither route is guaranteed.
  3. Candidate revision: Ivo changes a relation in his own description, often the way he can be addressed or counted by the opposing condition. A proposed change may be incoherent.
  4. Resolution and testimony: The revision either resolves with the encounter or fails. A later witness may establish what changed, but testimony is evidence of a result, not the switch that makes every revision possible. Recognition cannot prove continuity of consciousness.
  5. Consequence: The local threat may cease to apply, while a friendship, obligation, or piece of evidence is lost. Every chosen change increases continuity debt.

This process is analyzed through the ten axioms. They are in-universe theory with objections, not externally proven laws.

Two timings of revision

Responsive refinement follows a surviving difference. Ivo sees the edge of a missing fact, identifies a relation he can change, and tries to continue. If the edge and every witness vanish before he acts, this route closes.

The Prior Answer is a harder, prospective form. Ivo meets a condition while choice is still possible and revises the terms of his own succession before the condition resolves. When the White Interval removed every later trace of the first Ivo, the prepared candidate resolved with the closing act. It did not wait for a survivor to discover a clue. The first Ivo was gone; the successor stopped the force's next act and could not certify that he had inherited the same consciousness. This route demands prior contact, a coherent candidate, and a resolution the opposing authority has not already foreclosed.[1]

The distinction matters in comparison: destroying all later evidence of Ivo does not establish that a revision already undertaken could not resolve. To show decisive closure, an opponent must also govern the preparation and resolution of that candidate in the actual encounter. A title such as “ultimate” does not supply the missing interaction by itself.

Beyond the archivists' sequence

The responsive and prospective routes are practices of Ivo's chosen, accountable life. The Unconditioned Draft identifies the Unruled Witness as the deeper source of Self-Refinement. At that peak, he need not wait for an opposing condition, preserve a witness, occupy an external substrate, or borrow a local logic to make revision possible. The archivists could require those things to verify one of his changes; they could not thereby make their verification procedure a law over the being who first wrote Ivo's life. The unanswered question of personal continuity remains a burden he chooses to take seriously, not a switch that turns his power off.

Eight kinds of edit

OperationWhat changesWhat a reader sees
Reality editingAn event inside an accountA flood takes another street.
Concept editingA category used by that accountRefugees can call a place home without owning it.
Logical editingPermitted inference or relationTwo incompatible rescue reports remain useful without making every statement true.
Narrative editingThe account connecting eventsA bell gains the cause missing from its consequences.
Meta-narrative editingThe relation between accounts and their readingsAn omission in a fable can testify in Leth.
Ontological editingWhat counts as existent in an accountAn unrecorded child becomes legible to a census.
Semantic editingWhat a statement addressesA total attack resolves against the defendant it actually named.
Self-editingThe description by which Ivo continues through the other operationsHis capacity or target relation changes, leaving him unsure who survived.

Self-editing is the central operation because all other edits presume an editor who persists across the change. It exposes the deepest cost rather than escaping it.

Absorbed opposition

Opposition → information → candidate revision → possible continuation. A stronger opponent may reveal a limitation. An opponent outside familiar narrative relations may reveal that the local hierarchy was not universal. A rival reviser can make Ivo's earlier solution obsolete. None of this means Ivo steals the opponent's powers. If there is no encounter from which to form a candidate, refinement is unestablished. If no witness can connect the resulting states, personal continuity remains unproved even when an action by a successor is recorded.

The address of an absolute attack

A claim that an effect destroys "anything" still needs a way to pick out what it destroys in the encounter. In the tribunal episode, the attack targeted a continuous defendant indexed by name, body, and proceeding. Ivo changed the relation by which a subsequent Ivo counted as that defendant. The attack executed against the indexed defendant. It did not weaken or fail. The question of whether the later Ivo is the same consciousness is left open. This does not imply that all absolute effects require the same sort of address.

Competing self-reference

If another entity also revises itself whenever surpassed, the interaction may escalate indefinitely, converge on a common fixed point, split into incomparable continuations, or remain undecidable. A fixed point requires shared rules under which both revisions stabilize. The archive does not assume those rules. Ivo's distinctive route is to inspect the comparison relation when it becomes part of the encounter; a rival could remove that route before he uses it. See Sovereign/Philosophy and The Reader Problem.

Mathematical inspiration and boundary

Diagonal arguments inspire the idea that a proposed complete list can expose an omitted construction under specified conditions. They do not prove a character escapes every possible list. Gödel's theorems concern particular formal systems and do not establish omnipotence. Fixed-point language is useful for rival revisers only when a well-defined operator and suitable conditions exist. Model theory helps distinguish a statement from its interpretation; Sovereign's ability to change that relation is fictional extrapolation.

Jurisdiction and uncertainty

An edit works inside an account or an established relation between accounts. Being named in a higher account is not automatically enough to control it. The White Interval demonstrates a prepared self-revision against a force closing every ordinary witness and account in the scene; its success is narrower than authority over every imaginable system. A genuinely complete authority that also controls the formation and resolution of Ivo's candidate could block him. Whether a particular opponent has done so must be established from the encounter, rather than inferred from an epithet.
