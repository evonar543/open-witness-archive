Sovereign/Comparative model

'''Sovereign's comparative model''' is an interpretation of the character's fictional architecture, not a canon verdict over other works. A comparison must first establish a meaningful encounter, compatible terms, jurisdiction, and a witnessable continuation. Stating that another character is "above" Ivo does not by itself establish those conditions; stating that Ivo revises does not establish that he survives them.

How a comparison is made

Character → selected evidence → interpretation → comparative model is the mapping used by an in-world archive and by readers. Selection can omit an anti-feat. Interpretation can confuse a local rule with a universal one. A model can rank a state without establishing that the rank is the last possible self-description. Ivo's in-story ability can examine such mapping assumptions when they are part of an encounter. It does not alter a real reader's conclusion by decree.

Categories of strong claims

Each entry gives the source of the claim's strength, why an ordinary opponent would lose, the possible Self-Refinement route, and the resulting status. The route is conditional on residue, jurisdiction, and continuance.

Difficult substrates and timings

A stronger opponent can defeat a current Ivo. The resulting difference may support a later revision only if some witness survives. An opponent outside conventional narrative layers requires an actual interaction relation; the word "outside" is insufficient. An opponent with no identity may still have effects, but Ivo cannot assume a targetable self. Information erasure may leave an edge around a missing fact; total removal of every edge would stop the known mechanism. A pre-causal attack can close before Ivo thinks; a noncausal witness might still register it, but no such witness is guaranteed.

An imperceptible opponent may be known by changes it causes; if it causes no accessible change, there is no evidenced encounter. An allegedly omnipotent opponent is evaluated at its actual or stipulated jurisdiction. A rival adapter receives the same chance to revise. An opponent who rewrites Ivo may produce a successor with no witnessable link, which is a genuine loss even if someone later uses his name. Narrative immunity may block edits to the opponent while leaving self-edits possible. Beyond-author claims require an account of what "author" means and what has been demonstrated.

A contradictory opponent is analyzed under its local inference rules; the archive does not infer anything from a contradiction. An indefinable opponent can sometimes be approached through effects but may make comparison undefined. A propertyless opponent that neither acts nor leaves a difference offers no contest; if it acts, the action is a relevant relation. An opponent outside logic may have no shared semantics with Ivo's account. Without translation, neither victory nor defeat is established.

The mirror reviser

Suppose another being's rule is "I automatically surpass anything that surpasses me." If both revision rules use the same ordering and preserve a common witness, responses may form an infinite sequence. A fixed point is possible only if the rules admit a stable joint state. If each rule negates the other's stability, deadlock or divergence follows. If "surpass" refers to different properties in each account, the beings may be incomparable. Ivo's best route is to revise the *meaning of comparison* as part of the encounter. That is a distinctive breadth of operation, not a proof of semantic dominance. The rival may do the same, or remove the shared relation before he can use it.

Essential Divinity case

The Essential Divinity comparison tests a difficult World of Darkness example. It gives Ivo a concrete victory condition—continuing as a distinct, answerable person after an attempted finalization of his agency—and derives a possible route from the mechanism already documented in this archive. It also identifies the assumptions under which that route fails. This is a hypothetical analysis, not a canon encounter or an unconditional verdict over another work.

The limits of non-finality

"No final description" sounds like a stable definition. The archive answers by treating non-finality as a procedure: when a proposed final description leaves a usable difference, another account may be attempted. The procedure has conditions and costs. It does not prove that every conceivable closure leaves a difference. This preserves the character's strongest defensible position without converting an interesting paradox into an automatic win clause.
