Sovereign/Axioms

The ten axioms are the strongest surviving in-universe theory of the observable Self-Refinement used by Ivo Ren. They were assembled by archivists after the Open Witness episodes. Each includes a possible objection. They describe the practices by which his chosen life could be examined; they are not compulsory laws over the Unruled Witness or over all fiction.

1. Address

Statement: An effect resolves against a target through an address supplied by its account. In plain terms, even "anything" must pick out what it acts on. In the tribunal, the Absolute Attack addressed the continuous defendant in its docket. Readdressing allowed a later Ivo to stand outside that docket relation while the effect destroyed its actual target. Consequence: Examining a target relation can be more useful than increasing durability. Problem: Some effects may have no separable address, or their address may cover every valid continuation.

2. Residue

Statement: A responsive revision needs an accessible difference. The bell had consequences with a missing cause; that gap gave Ivo his first route. A prospective revision may instead be formed before closure and resolve with it, as in The White Interval. Consequence: Erasing every later trace does not cancel an earlier candidate. Problem: A closure that also prevents preparation or governs the candidate's resolution may still succeed.

3. Revision

Statement: A witnessed difference can license a candidate change in Ivo's self-description; it does not guarantee a viable one. Ivo revised his capacity to read a lethal order because a witness retained a mismatch the order had not settled. Consequence: Adaptation is generative rather than a list of prewritten counters. Problem: A proposed continuation may contradict every available witness.

4. Continuance

Statement: A subsequent state counts as Ivo in the archive only through an accountable relation to a former state. A witness helps establish that relation; it is evidence, not always the cause of the revision. Teren's lost memory makes a revised Ivo harder to recognize even though Ivo remembers Teren. Consequence: Survival claims require more than matching memories. Problem: A perfect copy might satisfy every witness while failing to preserve first-person consciousness.

5. Jurisdiction

Statement: Every edit acts within an account or a specified relation among accounts. The Closed King remains absolute inside his book; Ivo's disputed route concerns the book's relation to a reader. Consequence: A local absolute need not be insulted to describe an exterior relation. Problem: A genuinely universal authority could include every relation.

6. Open Account

Statement: An account is not final merely because it describes itself as final. The hostile archivist's terminal definition still had assumptions that could be encountered. Consequence: A title such as "beyond authors" is a claim to examine, not an automatic result. Problem: This principle does not establish that a truly complete account cannot exist.

7. Symmetry

Statement: A rival reviser has the same prima facie claim to continuation as Ivo. When one rival learned Ivo's prior revision, each response became information to the other. Consequence: Recursive escalation, convergence, deadlock, or incomparability may follow. Problem: Without a shared comparison relation, "surpass" may be undefined.

8. Unborrowed Consent

Statement: Ability to change another person's identity is not itself permission. Ivo nearly revised Sana's memories under a rule that treated silence as consent and later adopted Counterfactual Mercy. Consequence: A person may reject a physically better outcome. Problem: Emergencies can make meaningful consent impossible.

9. Cost of Proof

Statement: No collection of reconstructed evidence proves an unbroken first-person life. The ferryman's complete memories did not tell Ivo whether he had restored the husband who drowned. Consequence: More accurate reconstruction does not close the identity problem. Problem: Ordinary personal identity may never have offered such proof either.

10. The Untouched Cup

Statement: Ivo shall not intentionally edit The Anchor Memory. The cup's color could be settled through revision; he refuses. Consequence: He retains one possible piece of unchosen evidence at personal cost. Problem: A false memory can also harm people, and preserving it may be another form of control.

Status of the theory

The axioms explain recorded events, but the record is incomplete and some testimony conflicts. The First Dialogue and the Severance account indicate that the archivists mistook their conditions of proof for Sovereign's absolute limits. See Sovereign/Feats for events, The Unconditioned Draft for the peak state, and Sovereign/Philosophy for the unresolved identity question.
