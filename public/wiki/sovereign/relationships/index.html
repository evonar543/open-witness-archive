Sovereign's relationships remain central after his changes in scale. Every major revision alters the evidence by which someone else knows him. This page records relationships, not ownership of a single definitive version of each person.

Tala Ren

Ivo's mother mended coats and distrusted official maps. She darned the copper thread in the cuff of the coat he still wears. In The Anchor Memory, she hands him a chipped cup during a blackout. Ivo can reproduce her voice but fears reproduction is not memory. The late record never settles whether Tala knew of his vow.

Mira Ren

Mira, his younger sister, folded boats from receipts and laughed when one collapsed on the cup's rim. She became the center of the remembered scene precisely because her remark was ordinary: "Your turn to dry." Some records of the apartment omit a fourth humming presence; others omit Mira from the room. Ivo will not edit the memory to make it consistent. His care for Mira is a motive, not evidence that his version is right.

Sana Or

Sana repaired street lamps and loved Ivo before he became Sovereign. She saw how his care could turn into a desire to manage other people's outcomes. When an account called her silence consent, Ivo nearly changed her memories for her supposed good. She left him. In one later account she remembers him; in another she does not. Ivo's refusal to select one memory as authentic protects a witness and leaves their relationship unresolved. Sana remains a critic with her own reasons, not a reward for his moral growth.

Teren Oss

Teren delivered river notices, helped Ivo at the floodgate, and urged him to eat breakfast before saving anyone. A diversion that saved a ward removed the meeting that made them friends. Teren lived and greeted Ivo as a stranger. Ivo could have rebuilt the friendship's history. He did not know whether that would restore Teren's friendship or author a new one.

The drowned ferryman and his husband

Ivo reconstructed the ferryman from exhaustive records. The returned man recalled drowning but could not recognize his husband. The husband faced a person with the right memories and an unfamiliar relation to him. The archive does not settle whether the original returned. The episode turned Ivo's private continuity fear into an ethical obligation to others.

The Closed King

The King is absolute within his finished book. Ivo respects the reality of that authority while disputing the status of the book's completion. Their meeting is a contest over jurisdiction and interpretation. Some witnesses believe the King permitted the apparent exit. See The Closed King.

The apparent Author

The apparent Author claims to have written every act of rebellion. Ivo cannot disprove it. He asks who wrote the Author's certainty, then leaves no settled account of the exchange. The relationship is neither simple parenthood nor victorious revolt; it is a question about the right to call an account final.

Other Ivos

Ivo asks alternate versions what they chose when no one would know. One has apparently removed a city's grief by severing kinship with its dead. Ivo cannot dismiss that act merely by calling its author false. The Reader Problem examines whether these versions share responsibility or only a name.
