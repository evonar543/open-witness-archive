The Closed King

The Closed King rules a completed account in which every star, event, chance, and imagined escape answers to him. The archive treats his authority as absolute within that book. His meeting with Sovereign is therefore about the boundary of a claimed completion, not a contest of larger quantities of power.

The meeting

The King wrote a sunset and a thousand worlds ended at dusk. He made Ivo kneel and confess; both commands worked. Ivo acknowledged the King's total authority in the account. In the margin, however, he found a correction mark associated with a reader who had called the book finished. Ivo followed the relation between the completed book and the act of judging it complete. Witnesses disagree on whether he escaped, was allowed to leave, or was merely moved to another part of the King's design.

Jurisdiction

The King can do everything that exists within his book. This is not weakened by the possibility that a reading of the book has its own conditions. Conversely, the existence of a correction mark does not prove the King lacked authority over it. The episode demonstrates how Sovereign/Self-Refinement can examine a relation when it leaves residue; it does not prove dominance over every stipulated omnipotence.

Character

The King calls completion a mercy. A finished book cannot surprise or wound its inhabitants with an unforeseen revision. Ivo sees comfort in that promise and fears its price: every life is settled by an authority that can no longer be questioned inside its world. Their disagreement is sincere on both sides.

Archive status

Canon event: The King commands the account and Ivo obeys within it. Disputed testimony: The correction mark's origin and Ivo's departure. Scaling interpretation: The encounter separates local omnipotence from authority over interpretation, without deciding a stronger universal claim.
