The Reader Problem

The Reader Problem names the discovery that different interpretations of the same account can produce different viable Sovereigns. It is not a claim that Ivo controls real readers. Within the fiction, each reading can preserve witnesses that the others lack.

The plurality

One reading remembers Ivo as a man who always refused coercive repairs. Another remembers an Ivo who removed a city's grief by severing its kinship with the dead. Both have testimony. Ivo cannot simply call the second false without examining the people who lived with its consequences. If he fuses the accounts, he chooses which witnesses lose their history.

Can he experience every version?

The record shows partial contact through differences shared across readings, not complete awareness of every interpretation. He can sometimes recognize a choice another Ivo made; he cannot always claim to have made it himself. A contradiction may yield information for Self-Refinement if its edges remain visible. It may also prevent a single continuation from being ratified. Plurality is a resource and a threat.

Why this matters

The problem turns a power question into responsibility. If the city without grief contains real people, Ivo cannot erase its version merely to restore his preferred self-image. If the Ivo who committed that act is him, he may owe repair. If it is a distinct person, he may still owe help to the harmed. Either way, "that was another draft" is not enough.

Relation to comparison systems

A tiering system maps a character through selected evidence and an interpretation into a comparative model. Such a model can describe a particular Ivo. It does not settle which readings are legitimate or whether a revision has a witnessable continuation. The archive therefore keeps feats, theory, and comparative conclusions separate. See Sovereign/Comparative model.

Unanswered question

Does refusing to choose an original preserve Ivo's humanity, or has he made "Ivo" a name for a collection that no single person can inhabit? The story offers no final answer.

One unindexed transcript begins with a question and ends without a sender. Its only surviving catalogue note calls it an unsigned reply.
