The Anchor Memory is Ivo Ren's recollection of a blackout in Tala Ren's kitchen. Rain moved down the window. Tala handed him a chipped cup to dry. Mira balanced a boat made from a receipt on its rim; the boat collapsed and Mira laughed. "Your turn to dry," she said.

The memory matters because nothing grand happened. Ivo's later capacities could recreate the room, the rain, the voices, and every physical mark on the cup. They could not tell him whether a recreated moment would be the moment he once lived. He returns to it after revisions that change his own properties. It is the one account of himself he has vowed not to intentionally edit.

Discrepancies

Ivo calls the cup green in some tellings and blue in others. He remembers a fourth person humming just outside the room, though the surviving household records list only Tala, Mira, and Ivo. An early print of the scene gives "Your turn to dry" in Ivo's own handwriting. The measure shows another hand beneath it, but the trace cannot be assigned securely. These differences may be ordinary memory errors, evidence of prior revisions, or evidence that the memory was authored later. The archive does not choose one.

The vow

Ivo will not intentionally change the memory, even if a hostile witness uses it to hurt him or an edit could settle its origin. His reason is not confidence that it is genuine. He wants to preserve at least one thing he may not have authored himself. The vow provides no proof. It is a way of declining to manufacture proof.

The cost

Sana Or argues that an untouched falsehood can shape another person's life. If the fourth voice belongs to someone erased, refusing to investigate could protect Ivo at that person's expense. Ivo distinguishes investigation from editing, but the line can be difficult to hold when observation itself changes an account. The memory remains both refuge and moral problem.

Archive status

Canon event: Ivo repeatedly reports the scene and acts under the vow. Disputed testimony: The cup's color, the fourth voice, and the underwritten hand. Unresolved interpretation: Whether the scene happened before Self-Refinement, after it, or to a different person.
